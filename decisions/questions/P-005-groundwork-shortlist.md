## What follows from the answers (the Chief of Staff's reading, for the research list and the plan; not the owner's words)
- What is waiting on the owner comes first, but always shown in the context of the branches, pull requests and commits around it (answer 1); the owner wants to act from the view (answer 4).
- Any single thing that needs the owner buzzes the phone (answer 2). Today the owner learns what waits on them mostly through Chief of Staff sessions, and items do sit longer than they should, mostly from the owner's own busyness (answer 3).
- One view across all projects by default, which condenses and expands by the projects in focus or with immediate needs (answer 5); everything is reachable, but not all at once (answer 7).
- The scope the owner describes is wider than the kickoff's "Waiting on you" side: the timeline replaces all of the core screens (answer 6), takes in all the related desk work (answer 11), and holds coding work, pull requests, research questions, sources, approvals and design mockups (answer 8). The owner expects to spend considerable time in it (answer 8). The plan's value target, scope and milestones must be set to that width, or the narrower scope put back to the owner; either way the scope goes to the owner for approval at the Design gate.
- The model's own steps (gate, decision, research, stop) get a second row in the key (answer 9). Milestones are not a filter row, but the view shows how work feeds into each milestone (answer 10). Both settle open questions the basis left to the owner; the basis itself is not changed by this record.
