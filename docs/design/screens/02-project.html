Screen 2 of 7: Project

Tap a project card on Home to get here. Modelled on your Lifesum screenshot: a big "Current status" title, coloured group headings, and one rounded row per phase, each with a status word and a status circle. Tap a phase to see its milestones in plain English.

What's on this screen, and why

  • Lifesum's layout, used for phases. Lifesum's big "Current status" title becomes yours. The phases are listed strictly in order, Discovery, Design, Build, Launch, then Done, which is not a phase but where the project stands once you pass the Launch gate. The current phase is not lifted to the top: it stays in its place and is marked there, with a gold bar down its left edge, a gold hairline round its tile, a quiet raised edge and a small NOW tag by its name. Every row keeps its state word (finished, under way, not started, not yet). Operate and Review are the product's own after the Launch gate, so the desk doesn't show them. Each phase gets one rounded row: an icon, its name in caps, a status word, and a status circle (a filled tick, a gold ring, or an empty ring), so the state never depends on colour alone.
  • Two levels, no more. Tap a phase and its milestones open underneath in plain English: first the model's standard milestones for that phase (a count, tap for the list), then this project's own, each with its status and one short reason. The phase that's under way opens by default. A standard milestone shows only what a record shows, so most of Service Desk's read "not recorded". Going deeper, into each milestone's steps, is the timeline (screen 4).
  • The line under the title says what's happening in one sentence. Directly under it, two buttons jump to what needs you (the queue, screen 6) and who has the ball (the team, screen 3), so they're never more than one tap away.
  • Order is the operating model's, not the order things happened. Service Desk came back to Design after Build, so Design shows as Design i2, its second run, with one line about Design i1, and Build stays Finished.
  • A blocker shows where it bites. Launch carries "must pass first: the scrubbing proof", in coral, so you can see what's holding it before it starts.
  • Every item is real Service Desk data. The ⓘ explains the four phases and Done; underlined words explain themselves.
