## 1. Scope
*What is in, what is explicitly out, and where it must work (target devices or places).*

An **event** is one recorded step of the agent team's work: a GitHub object (a branch, commit, pull request, check, review, merge or deploy), or a step of the operating model that has no GitHub counterpart (a phase gate, a decision, research, a stop). The **timeline** is the desk's view of every event across the owner's projects, drawn like a CI run: kinds by shape, states by colour, who by name, as the basis sets out.

In, as the owner's answers set it (decisions/questions/P-005-groundwork-shortlist.md):
- One timeline across all projects by default, which condenses and expands by the projects in focus or with something that needs the owner (answer 5).
- What waits on the owner comes first, shown in the context of the branches, pull requests and commits around it (answer 1).
- The owner acts from the timeline: answering, approving and merging, each as the owner's own action (answer 4).
- Any single thing that needs the owner notifies their phone (answer 2).
- Everything can be reached, back through the whole life of the work, but not all at once (answer 7).
- The key's seven GitHub kinds, and a second row for the model's own steps: gate, decision, research, stop (answer 9).
- How each piece of work feeds its milestones is shown; milestones are not a filter row (answer 10).
- Code, pull requests, research questions, sources, approvals and design mockups, all reachable from their events (answer 8).
- The timeline replaces the desk's core screens (answer 6).
- The related desk work it takes in (answer 11): the check-status mark on each card; the pull-request briefing page, whose Discovery's research (research/Q-016-memo.md to research/Q-021-memo.md) this plan builds on; the desk follow-ups the owner put into one later piece of desk work (shumpmaster/Model-Lab decisions/questions/P-001-desk-follow-ups.md); and the three later items, an ask-or-jot box, upkeep fixes, and the portfolio's pull requests on the desk (intake/later-work.md).

Rules the timeline keeps:
- Records only, never guessing: an event or field the records do not hold shows as "not recorded", and a reconstructed step is marked as reconstructed.
- Waiting is never shown as a fault or as stalled.
- The owner's buttons act only as the owner; production deploys stay the owner's own approval.
- The view shows its as-of time, and says so when it or its feed has stopped.

Out:
- The Orchestrator's routing, the operating model and how agents work: unchanged, except where research finds a record must be added, which goes to the owner as its own change.
- The card fix (P-003) and the home-screen widget (P-004): each keeps its own plan and gates.

Where it must work: on the desk, on the owner's phone first and their computer, in the browsers the desk already supports, through its preview site and then its live address.

## 2. Strategy
*In plain language: the chain from the deliverables to the value — how this work produces it.*

Today the owner mostly learns what waits on them by coming into a session with the Chief of Staff and reading what is asked, why and in what order, and things sit longer than they should (decisions/questions/P-005-groundwork-shortlist.md, answer 3). The desk's screens list items in groups, apart from the branches, pull requests and commits they belong to. If the desk shows every event of the agents' work on one timeline, with what needs the owner first and in its context, and lets the owner act there, the owner sees and clears what waits on them sooner (V7), misses none of it (V8), and can trust what is shown because every event comes from a record (V9).

## 6. Value and stop rule
*This project's own measure of value, the target and date, and what counts as a miss — including the cost to the owner's time. Write each value target on its own line starting with its ID, like `V1: <measure>, <target>, <date>`; spec criteria trace to these IDs.*

*Draft targets, for the owner to approve, change or replace at the Design gate; never set by proxy.*

V7: Owner wait: the median time from a step starting to wait on the owner to the owner acting on it, at most half its baseline measured by experiment E1 before the Build gate, over the first 30 days after launch
V8: Missed steps: steps waiting on the owner that the timeline did not show within 5 minutes of their record, 0 over the first 30 days after launch
V9: Faithful: events the timeline shows that their records do not support (a wrong kind, state, who or time, or an event with no record), 0 in a weekly check against the records over the first 30 days after launch

V7 to V9 are numbered after the desk's V1 to V6 so that no two of the desk's targets share a name. A miss: V7 above half its baseline; any V8 or V9 count above 0. The cost to the owner's time: none to run them; the Chief of Staff runs the weekly V9 check and reports each miss.

Stop rule: work stops and comes back to the owner before anything more is done if experiment E2 shows that the records cannot carry the timeline without record changes the owner does not approve; if the access research shows acting from the timeline needs spend or a credential the owner does not approve, in which case acting drops out and the rest goes on only on the owner's word; or if a third readiness or plan-review round fails.

# Part 2 — The intent

## Intent
The owner's purpose, preferences and trade-offs for this project, and what they would hate. Drafted by
the Chief of Staff from conversations, portfolio defaults and past rulings; confirmed on the
move-to-Design card.

- **Purpose.** The owner's words: redraw the desk's "Waiting on you" side "as one timeline of everything the agent team does, read like a CI"; "I imagine an interface where I can see the coding work, the series of PRs, research questions, sources, approvals, design mockups etc… all captured within the format of this dynamic CI timeline" (decisions/questions/P-005-groundwork-shortlist.md, answer 8).
- **Preferences.** What waits on the owner first, "but critically i want to see it all in the context of all of the different branches, PRs, commits etc." (answer 1); act from the view (answer 4); "Any single thing that needs me" notifies (answer 2); one view across all projects that "seamlessly condenses and expands" (answer 5); "see everything - but not necessarily at one time" (answer 7).
- **Trade-offs.** The owner expects to spend "a considerable amount of time" in it if done well (answer 8), so depth matters more than a glance-only view.
- **What the owner would hate** (from the basis and the map's traps, to be confirmed): guessed events, waiting shown as failure, and a view that quietly stops while looking current.
