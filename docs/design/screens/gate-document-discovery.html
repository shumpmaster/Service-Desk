How a gate document works

  • Plain English first. Before any section, one paragraph says in plain words what went wrong or is still open, what changes for you, and whether any of it is irreversible.
  • Every gate has the same shape, on one page: nine numbered sections, in this order:
    1. the ask, with the recommendation;
    2. the vision;
    3. what this phase taught us;
    4. the plan for the next phase;
    5. the risks;
    6. cost and limits;
    7. what would make us stop;
    8. what you're agreeing to;
    9. the phase record, folded away, with the phase's milestones as the records show them.
  • Two voices, and the user's. The Chief of Staff writes the case for proceeding. The Critic writes a separate, independent view and checks that every claim in the case is backed by a record. You see both before you rule. In this mock-up, both voices are written from the records: the Critic's real verdict was a pass on round 4.
    • The user representative would play the project's target user and say approve or reject, marked as a simulation, not real users. You are Service Desk's only user, so its line reads not needed: "The user representative: not needed. The brief records that the owner is the user (docs/PROJECT.md, section 9)."
  • Four answers:
    • Proceed: pass the gate.
    • Hold: not yet. Nothing moves; the case is updated and the Critic reads it again. Give a reason.
    • Recycle: work a phase through again as its next iteration, such as Discovery i2, with a reason. At this gate only Discovery can be recycled.
    • Kill: close the project. Only you can write it.
    The older words still work: here, "build", "re-aim" and "stop".
  • Honest by rule.
    • Each line can be tapped to show the record it rests on.
    • Risks only carry a rating when a record supports one, so none here do.
    • Dates and figures say "proposed" or "estimate" where the brief does.
  • Visuals where they help: where you are in the desk's four phases, with this gate marked and Done at the end, today's way of working against the desk's, how the plan fits the free plan's limits (with the unmeasured one hatched, not guessed), how the deliverables depend on each other, your time caps, and, in the record at the back, the research and checks. Every visual is drawn from the same records as the text, so none adds a figure of its own.
  • The Build and Launch gates also show the join-up review, as a "does it fit together" finding and a risk.
