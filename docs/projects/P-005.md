## 1. Scope
*What is in, what is explicitly out, and where it must work (target devices or places).*

An **event** is one recorded step of the agent team's work: a GitHub object (a branch, commit, pull request, check, review, merge or deploy), or a step of the operating model that has no GitHub counterpart (a phase gate, a decision, research, a stop). The **timeline** is the desk's view of every event across the owner's projects, drawn like a CI run: kinds by shape, states by colour and by word, who by name, as the basis sets out. Section 8 defines the other terms.

- What waits on the owner comes first, shown in the context of the branches, pull requests and commits around it (answer 1).
- The owner acts from the timeline: answering, approving and merging, each recorded as the owner's own action (answer 4).
- Any single thing that needs the owner notifies their phone (answer 2).
- Everything can be reached, back through the whole life of the work, but not all at once (answer 7).
- The key's seven GitHub kinds, and a second row for the model's own steps: gate, decision, research, stop (answer 9).

How they are measured:
- V7: a wait starts and ends at these record times: a decision card, when P-003's rule shows it (computed by rerunning that rule on the dispatch log and the notes, since no record holds it), to its answer commit; a question, its file's pull request opened, to its answer record; a gate, its case filed, to the ruling; a pull request that needs the owner, ready, to merged; a deploy, requested, to approved. Steps that start waiting within the 30 days count; one still open at the end counts at its wait so far, as V5 does. Whether a proxy action counts is put to the owner at the Design gate. - V8: counted from the notification sender's run logs on GitHub Actions (kept 90 days by default, which covers the 30) and a log of when the timeline was open, kept on the owner's device and committed weekly from a prefilled link, as V1's was; the timeline keeps that log and link (D3). - V9: each week a reviewer who did not build the timeline checks a sample (20 events and every step that waited on the owner) against the records by hand, and counts any wrong kind, state, who or time, any event no record supports, any reconstructed step not marked as one, and any "not recorded" field shown with a value.

A miss: V7 above half its baseline; any V8 or V9 count above 0. The cost to the owner's time: none for V7 and V9; V8's device log costs the owner one commit a week from a prefilled link. The Chief of Staff runs the checks and reports each miss to the owner the same week.

# Part 2 — The intent

## Intent
The owner's purpose, preferences and trade-offs for this project, and what they would hate. Drafted by
the Chief of Staff from conversations, portfolio defaults and past rulings; confirmed on the
move-to-Design card.

- **Purpose.** The owner's words: redraw the desk's "Waiting on you" side "as one timeline of everything the agent team does, read like a CI"; "I imagine an interface where I can see the coding work, the series of PRs, research questions, sources, approvals, design mockups etc… all captured within the format of this dynamic CI timeline" (decisions/questions/P-005-groundwork-shortlist.md, answer 8).
- **Preferences.** What waits on the owner first, "but critically i want to see it all in the context of all of the different branches, PRs, commits etc." (answer 1); act from the view (answer 4); "Any single thing that needs me" notifies (answer 2); one view across all projects that "seamlessly condenses and expands" (answer 5); "see everything - but not necessarily at one time" (answer 7).

- **What the owner would hate** (from the basis and the map's traps, to be confirmed): guessed events, waiting shown as failure, and a view that quietly stops while looking current.
