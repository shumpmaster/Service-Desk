Pattern: show a few most-important items first; put detail one level down behind a label that sets expectations; show when data was last updated if people check more often than it refreshes.
- Nielsen 2006: "Initially, show users only a few of the most important options"; label the link so expectations match; systems with more than two levels lose usability (paraphrase, exact sentence not returned).
- Apple Disclosure controls: "Use a disclosure control to hide details until they're relevant."
- Apple Widgets: "Create a layout that provides essential information at a glance and allows people to view additional details by taking a longer look"; "If people are likely to check your widget more frequently than you can update it, consider displaying text that describes when the data was last updated." Widgets are not dashboards.
- Budiu 2020 defines information scent only; its "misleading labels erode trust" claim was not confirmed.
"Exactly one step" is the memo's opinion, resting on Nielsen's two-level point.
