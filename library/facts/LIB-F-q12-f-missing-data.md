Stated as the survey's report (Alsufyani, Forshaw & Fernstad, 2024), not as a finding confirmed from the originals:
- Citing Song & Szafir: "highlighting the missing data and local linear imputation resulted in higher perceived confidence and data quality"; approaches "that break the visual continuity of a graph reduce the perception of quality in data and can bias interpretation".
- Citing Eaton et al.: "users may not notice that data is missing when it is replaced by a default value".
- The Colorado page confirms only the scope of Song & Szafir's study (imputation and visualization methods, perceived confidence and data quality, line and bar charts), not the findings.
Single survey, so it does not count as two sources. Original papers were not read.
