# Ruling needed: Eight short questions about keeping up with changes

Milestone: Discovery-3
Asked in session: session_01QBwHGLrbUaowxNjhF5oC3A

WHY: You asked for a page with every pull request summarized "almost like a briefing ppt", and chose to start with a small Discovery (the early research stage). Its first step mapped what an expert would ask before deciding whether you need anything beyond what you have now. Eight of those questions only you can answer. Some say "if a summary…": that is to learn your view, not to assume one will be built, and no answer commits you to building anything. Your answers are kept as your own words and used to set the research. About ten minutes; a sentence or two each; "skip" is a fine answer. Reply with numbers, for example "1. …, 3. skip". If you don't answer, nothing breaks and nothing is built: the Discovery waits, and the research list stays unset until you do.

  1. How do you find out today that a change has merged or is waiting on you, and about how long does that take you in a week?
  2. Can you remember a change you missed, saw late, or merged without enough understanding? What was it?
  3. For a change waiting on you, what do you need to see to decide without opening GitHub?
  4. For changes already merged, do you want to know about each one, only the ones that went wrong or surprised you, or none?
  5. Would you rather get a short list when you open the desk, a once-a-day or once-a-week note, a phone alert, a mix, or nothing beyond what you have now?
  6. What would "I have dealt with this" mean for you (looked at it, decided, nothing more), and should the desk remember it across your phone and computer?
  7. If a short summary were wrong or left out a risk, what would you do about it, and would you still want it if it carried a visible "not checked" label?
  8. Are you willing to let the text of your pull requests and changes go to an outside AI service to write summaries, and is any project too private for that? (Service-Desk's repository is already public; the answer matters for the others.)
