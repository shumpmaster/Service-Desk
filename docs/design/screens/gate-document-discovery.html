Gate document: Discovery → Design

The document behind a proceed-or-kill decision. It sells the vision, the plan and the risk, and the record of work done is folded away at the back. This is Service Desk's real gate of 5 October, rewritten from its project brief (Part 1, draft 4) and the phase's records. You ruled "build" (proceed) at 17:14.

How a gate document works

  • Every gate has the same shape: the ask at the top, then eight numbered sections:
    1. the vision;
    2. what this phase taught us;
    3. the plan from here;
    4. the risks;
    5. cost and limits;
    6. what would make us stop;
    7. what you're agreeing to;
    8. the phase record, folded away.
  • Two voices. The Chief of Staff writes the case for proceeding. The Critic writes a separate, independent view and checks that every claim in the case is backed by a record. You see both before you rule. In this mock-up, I wrote both voices from the records: the Critic's real verdict was a pass on round 4.
  • Four answers:
    • Proceed.
    • Hold: stay and finish what's open.
    • Recycle: go back into an earlier phase as a numbered iteration, with your reason.
    • Kill: close the project. Only you can choose it.
  • Honest by rule.
    • Each line can be tapped to show the record it rests on.
    • Risks only carry a rating when a record supports one, so none here do.
    • Dates and figures say "proposed" or "estimate" where the brief does.
  • Visuals where they help: where you are in the six phases, today's way of working against the desk's, how the plan fits the free plan's limits (with the unmeasured one hatched, not guessed), how the deliverables depend on each other, your time caps, and, in the record at the back, the research and checks. Every visual is drawn from the same records as the text, so none adds a figure of its own.
  • The Design and Build gates also show the join-up review, as a "does it fit together" finding and a risk.
