# Ruling needed: GitHub now deletes its run results after 90 days; should the timeline keep copies for the whole life of the work, and if so, where?

Milestone: Discovery-7, M6, M7

WHY: You want to reach everything, back through the whole life of the work (your answer 7). GitHub's pages say that from 1 October 2026 it deletes the results of its automated runs and checks, and the pass-or-fail marks on each change, once they pass each repository's setting, 90 days by default. Whether the deleting has already started rests on one GitHub page, which your ruling on vendor pages says no decision may rest on; this card does not need it, since GitHub's pages agree the deleting applies from that date either way. To show older results, the timeline must copy them before they go, into your repositories or onto your Cloudflare account, where the desk already runs. You decide how far back the timeline reaches and what data may leave your repositories; part of this cannot be undone, since a result deleted before it is copied is gone.
How to answer: one letter.
If you don't answer: the card stays open and does not default; while it waits nothing is copied. If the deleting has started, results from about mid-August on pass 90 days day by day from now, and are lost for good. Other work on the timeline goes on.

Background:
- A copy would hold, in the records research's opinion, who acted, when, the result, the run's number and the change it ran on; what a copy can read from GitHub is checked in Design. The research estimates that links to GitHub's own page for a run stop working once it passes the setting.
- Service-Desk and Model-Lab are public and keep these results at most 90 days; the private portfolio and operating-model repositories can be set to keep them up to 400 days.

OPTIONS:
  A. 90 days, with no copies — the timeline shows each result while GitHub keeps it, then shows it as no longer kept. Gain: nothing new is stored or run anywhere, and nothing changes in how your projects keep records. Cost: runs and checks older than 90 days show only as "no longer kept", short of your answer 7, and cannot be recovered. The files in your repositories are not touched by the deleting, in the research's estimate; how long GitHub keeps pull requests and reviews is not in the research read. It rules out showing older results, except in the private repositories if you raise their setting.
  B. The whole life, as files in the repositories — each result is copied, soon after its run ends, into a file saved in its own repository, which the deleting does not touch, in the research's estimate. Gain: older results stay on the timeline for the work's whole life, and nothing is stored at Cloudflare. Cost: the desk holds no key that can write, so in the Chief of Staff's estimate the copies would be written by an automated job in each repository, using GitHub Actions time and adding saved copies to each repository's history; that is a change to the records your projects keep, made in the operating model, the rules all your projects share, and it comes to you as its own ruling, so choosing B commits you to a second decision, and until you approve it nothing is copied, as under A; the research gives no cost figure, so your plan's $0 ceiling holds unless you approve more. It rules out keeping the copies outside GitHub.
  C. The whole life, at Cloudflare — a new program on your Cloudflare account, run on a schedule, reads each result before it is deleted and copies it into Cloudflare's storage. Gain: older results stay on the timeline, and nothing changes in the repositories or in how your projects keep records. Cost: copies of your project records, from the private repositories as well as the public ones, are stored at Cloudflare; which kind of storage is chosen in Design, and one kind does not say in which country it keeps data while another can be held to the EU or the US; the desk's present hosting cannot run anything on a schedule, by Cloudflare's pages as last read, so this needs a second kind of Cloudflare program, holding the desk's read-only key, which in the Chief of Staff's reading of the plan's limits comes to you to approve before it is set up; with that key the copies would lack check results, and possibly the pass-or-fail marks, from the private repositories, unless you choose a GitHub App, a separate read-only connection to GitHub, on the reading question; the research gives no cost figure, so your plan's $0 ceiling holds unless you approve more; the record of the work then lives partly outside your repositories. It rules out keeping everything in GitHub.
RECOMMENDATION: B, with low-to-moderate confidence — it meets "see everything" while keeping every record in your repositories, with nothing stored at Cloudflare; its cost, like C's, is not yet known. What would change it: if you would rather not change the records your projects keep, choose A, or C if older results matter more to you than where they are stored.
RISK CLASS: R0 (records only: the plan is redrafted to match; nothing is copied or stored by this answer)   REVERSIBILITY: irreversible in part: the plan's choice can be changed later, but each result that passes 90 days without a copy, under A or while the card waits, cannot be recovered   BLAST RADIUS: the timeline's draft plan: how far back it reaches, its data limit, and under B a records change put to you on its own; nothing on the desk changes now
DEFAULT: none — irreversible questions never default (AGENTS.md §5), and the plan keeps its data limit for you to rule (docs/projects/P-005.md, section 5)   TIMEOUT: none

Not offered: copies kept for a limited time, such as a year: under B or C how long copies are kept can be set in Design, so it is not a choice of its own. Raising the private repositories' setting alone, which reaches 400 days, not the whole life, and does not cover Service-Desk or Model-Lab; it can be done under any option. Copies on your devices alone, which would not reach back past the day each device first opened the timeline.
