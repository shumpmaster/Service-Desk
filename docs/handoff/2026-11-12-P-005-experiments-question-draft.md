# Ruling needed: should the timeline's two record-reading experiments run during Design?

Milestone: Design-3, M6

WHY: The timeline's plan rests on two unknowns that research cannot settle, because they are about this setup's own records: whether they hold enough to draw every kind of event the timeline shows, and how long the things that wait on you wait today. Two experiments answer them during Design. The rules let an unknown stay open past Discovery only with your recorded ruling, so until you answer, the plan fails its readiness check on this point and cannot go to its Design gate. The plan's first Design milestone (that the records can carry the timeline) rests on the records experiment, and the draft target for how long things wait on you is a rule, half of how long they wait today, which only the waiting experiment can turn into a number. It comes to you, not to your proxy, because options B and C change the waiting target and the stop rule, which are yours, and the readiness standard needs your own ruling. Answering now also settles the item on taking these experiments into Design that the plan lists for your Design gate; leaving out the third experiment the map suggested stays on that list.

The two experiments, as the plan drafts them. Both only read records and change nothing; a read-only helper runs them, and the Reviewer checks its findings:
- The records experiment (E2): it rebuilds one finished piece of work end to end from its records alone and lists what cannot be rebuilt, including whether old run records still open.
- The waiting experiment (E1): it reads the records of the 30 days before it closes and times each wait by the same rule as the draft waiting target (decision cards, questions, gates, pull requests that need you, deploys). It looks back over past records, so it adds no 30-day trial to your time.

OPTIONS:
  A. Run both during Design — they are registered at Design's experiment step, each with the fallback the plan gives: if the waiting experiment cannot time a kind of wait, that kind is left out of both it and the waiting target; if no kind can be timed, the waiting target comes back to you as a fixed number with no baseline; anything the records experiment cannot rebuild shows as "not recorded". Cost to you, during Design: each gap it finds comes to you, to approve a record change or accept that it shows as "not recorded", before the first Design milestone is reached; the work stops and comes back to you if the records experiment finds one of the key's eleven kinds, or a step that waits on you, with no record of its time and who, or if the waiting experiment finds fewer than 10 waits or a median under an hour; and at the Design gate you are asked to extend your route ruling, which allows record changes only where Discovery shows them needed, into Design. It rules out designing the timeline before knowing what the records hold.
  B. Run only the records experiment (E2) during Design — the records question is answered as in A, with A's costs except the stop-rule return for too few or too short waits, which no longer applies. The waiting target's "half of today's wait" and its miss no longer apply either; the target comes back to you at the Design gate as a fixed number with no baseline. Before the readiness check, the Chief of Staff takes the unknown "how long the owner's steps wait today" out of the plan's list of unknowns and redrafts the waiting target as a fixed number. Cost: a fixed number set without knowing today's waits. It rules out setting the waiting target against how long things wait today.
  C. Run neither — Design goes ahead on the research alone. The waiting target changes as in B, and the stop rule's clauses on both experiments lapse. The plan's unknown "whether this setup's records hold what an event record must" then has no test, and the readiness check cannot pass with an unknown that has none; so before that check the Chief of Staff rewrites the plan to drop that unknown, and rewords the first Design milestone, which asks that every kind of event be known to have a record, a thing the research cannot show for this setup. Both rewrites come to you with the plan at the Design gate. Saves: no record-reading during Design, and no gap approvals, stop-rule returns or request to extend your route ruling then. Cost: the gaps are only put off, and come back, with any record change they need, if they are found in Build. It rules out the plan's check that the records can carry the timeline before it is designed.
RECOMMENDATION: A — both answer questions about this setup's own records that research cannot, and the first Design milestone and the waiting target each rest on one of them.
