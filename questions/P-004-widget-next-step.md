# Ruling needed: The widget test couldn't reach what it was meant to check. What next?

Milestone: none (the widget's milestone, M5, is still only a proposal in its plan, so nothing here serves a milestone yet)
Asked in session: session_01QBwHGJ3UVJ6HY4SS92w7UW

WHY: The widget test ran on your phone for a day and came back "not judged": neither a pass nor a fail. Inside the Scriptable app, the first full read finished, and a later refresh matched the desk's own summary exactly. But on the home screen, all 25 of the widget's own refreshes were stopped by the phone within seconds, and none of them started from the copy the app had kept. The likely reason is that the home-screen widget can't see the folder where the app kept that copy (its Caches folder), while it can see the folder that holds the log (Documents). So the step the test was built to measure, a home-screen refresh starting from a kept copy, never happened. The test's own rules say to run it once more after a "not judged", but run unchanged it should hit the same wall. Until you rule, no more work is done on the widget. Nothing the desk does or shows changes, whichever you choose.
OPTIONS:
  A. "Second test, kept copy in Documents (Recommended)" — "The Builder moves the kept copy into Documents, the folder the widget can see, and the test is written down again and checked before it runs. Documents is copied by your phone's iCloud backup, which your Phone only rule doesn't allow, so for the test you switch Scriptable off in the phone's iCloud Backup settings. That also removes Scriptable's scripts from your backups, and people report the switch can turn itself back on, so you check it again at the end. The test data comes back by the route you allowed last time, which choosing this extends to the second test. Your time: about 15 minutes to set up, a day of normal use, about 10 minutes at the end. It may still fail: in the app a later refresh took 30 seconds to over 2 minutes, while the widget's attempts were stopped after 2 to 4 seconds. Whether the real widget may keep its copy this way is for the Design gate."
  B. "Sender saves a summary" — "The widget route counts as failed. P-004 takes the first fallback you were offered: the desk's notification sender also saves a summary, and the widget only reads that. It changes the sender, which needs your approval, and where the summary is saved has to keep your private projects out of the desk's public repository. Not sized yet."
  C. "Counts-only widget" — "The widget route counts as failed. P-004 takes the other fallback: a widget that reads a few counts straight from GitHub and doesn't show the desk's own summary. No desk change; the lightest to build. You lose what you chose the widget for: the desk's summary at a glance."
  D. "Repeat the test as it is" — "Run the same test once more, as its rules say after a not-judged result. Another day of your phone and about 25 minutes of your time. Expect the same result, because the widget still can't see the kept copy; if it is not judged again, this question comes back."
RECOMMENDATION: A, because it is the only option that can still tell whether the widget you chose can work, it costs about a day of your phone and half an hour of your time, and both fallbacks stay open if it fails. My confidence is medium: the widget may also be stopped for time or memory even with the kept copy, and nothing measured so far rules that out. It would change if you'd rather not touch your backup settings; then C is the quickest working widget.
