# Ruling needed: When the desk cannot read its records, should it hide decision cards and say so, show the ones that have notes, or show them at once when only the raise times are missing?

Milestone: M4

WHY: Something failed: the Critic has failed the card fix's new spec at both of its reviews, and the second time its first reason was this point. The next review is the third, and a third failure stops the work and brings it back to you. The plan says that when the desk has not read the record of when each card was raised, a decision card shows at once. Your re-specify ruling says a card is held or shown only from the records on main, so every view and the sender agree. The two cannot both hold: a card shown because one read missed that record would be hidden again by the next read, and the desk and the notifications could disagree. The rewritten spec follows your ruling, and its writer marks this as needing your answer before the spec is frozen. The plan was approved at its gate, so changing it needs your ruling. All the options can be undone. If you do not answer within 72 hours, I adopt my recommendation, B.
OPTIONS:
  A. Hide every decision card and say so — while a read of the desk or of the notification sender cannot get its records (the queue, the notes or the record of when each card was raised), it shows no decision card and sends no notice for one, and the queue and Home say which record could not be read. Once it is read, each card is held or shown by its real age. This fix does not change how gate cards, questions and pull requests behave. This is close to what the rewritten spec says now; the spec still needs one fix under it, since it counts these cards as misses, which the records cannot show.
     Cost: for as long as the records cannot be read, you see no decision card at all, only the line, with no time limit, which makes an exception to the plan's promise that no card is held for good and to your preference that none is hidden for more than a day; how often or for how long that happens is not known. The weekly value check cannot count these as misses, because a failed read leaves no record behind, so the plan's meaning of a miss is narrowed to leave them out; the line on the desk at the time is the only sign. Rules out: a card shown and then hidden with no reason given, and views that disagree.
  B. Show the cards that have notes, hide the rest and say so — as A, except that when only the record of when cards were raised is missing, a decision card whose Chief of Staff notes with a recommended word are on main is treated as if that record had been read: it shows with its recommendation, is counted on Home and the board, can be answered, and its notice is sent, since none of that depends on when it was raised. Only the cards without notes are hidden, and the line says that decision cards without notes are not shown.
     Cost: as A, but only for cards without notes; of those, the ones within their first day would be held anyway, so what you lose during an outage is the cards past their day or with no recorded raise time. Every option needs the spec changed before its third review, and B adds the most new wording: the rule for a read without the records, the line's wording and the join sheets, all new wording going into the review whose failure stops the work. Rules out: the same as A.
  C. Show them all at once, as the plan says now — this covers only the record of when cards were raised, as the plan does; a read that cannot get the queue or the notes is left to the spec. A card whose raise time has not been read shows straight away, marked "No Chief of Staff recommendation yet", and may be hidden again on the next read.
     Cost: a card can show and then vanish, and the views and the notifications can disagree for a while, which sets aside part of your re-specify ruling; the spec is rewritten again. Rules out: a card hidden for longer than a day because the record of when cards were raised cannot be read; a card can still be hidden that long if the queue or the notes cannot be read, as the spec now has it.
RECOMMENDATION: B — of the two choices that keep your ruling that every view and the sender agree, it hides fewer cards, and the ones you most need, with my recommendation, still show; the desk says why the others are missing. Confidence: medium; it would change to A if you would rather send the simplest wording into the review whose failure stops the work, or to C if never missing a card matters more to you than the views agreeing.
