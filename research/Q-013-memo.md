**Labels.** Each row is a **fact** (what a source says), an **estimate**, or an **opinion** (design advice from the source). ## 1. Progressive disclosure for glanceable overviews

| # | Statement | Kind | Label / grade | Source and quote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Show the most important things first and the rest on request. Everything users often need belongs up front. | opinion | **Corroborated** (Nielsen; Apple, a different author), A | Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure", NN/g, 2006-12-03: "Initially, show users only a few of the most important options. Offer a larger set of specialized options upon request." and "You must disclose everything that users frequently need up front, so that they have to progress to the secondary display only on rare occasions." https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/ — Apple HIG Widgets: "Create a layout that provides essential information at a glance and allows people to view additional details by taking a longer look." https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets |
| 1.2 | A glance surface needn't show everything; a tap opens the detail. | opinion | Guideline (single authoritative source), A | Apple HIG Live Activities: "Your Live Activity doesn't need to display everything. … When a person wants to learn more, they can tap your Live Activity to open your app where you can provide additional detail." https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities |
| 1.3 | A tap on the glance item should open the matching detail directly, not a general screen. | opinion | Guideline (single authoritative source), A | Apple HIG Widgets: "Deep link to details and actions that directly relate to the widget's content, and don't make people navigate to the relevant area in the app." (same Widgets page) |
| 1.4 | How many levels: more than two levels usually has low usability. | opinion | **Single source**, B | Nielsen 2006: "In practice, designs that go beyond 2 disclosure levels typically have low usability because users often get lost when moving between the levels." No second source found. |

| 1.6 | A one-line text summary above detail helps people grasp the key point at a glance. | opinion | Guideline (single authoritative source), A | Apple HIG Charting data: "You can also display brief descriptive text that serves as a headline or summary for a chart, helping people grasp essential information at a glance. ## 2. Unknown, stale or estimated values

| # | Statement | Kind | Label / grade | Source and quote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Use zero only for a true zero. Mark unavailable, not-applicable, estimated, provisional and low-reliability values with distinct marks. The guidance is for statistical tables. | opinion (published style guidance) | **Single source**, B | UK Government Analysis Function, "Symbols in tables", 2022-01-11: "A zero or '0' should only be used when a data point is a true zero." Shorthand: "e = estimated … Use this shorthand to show when a data point is estimated"; "x = not available"; "z = not applicable"; "p = provisional"; "u = low reliability". https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/symbols-in-tables-definitions-and-help/ |
| 2.2 | Readers take a plotted point as exact unless its uncertainty is shown. Graded intervals help show that a value is uncertain. | opinion (textbook) | **Single source**, B | Claus O. Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, ch. "Visualizing uncertainty": "when we see a data point drawn in a specific location, we tend to interpret it as a precise representation of the true data value". https://clauswilke.com/dataviz/visualizing-uncertainty.html |
| 2.3 | If people may check more often than the data can update, show when it was last updated. Don't hide stale data behind placeholders. | opinion | Guideline (single authoritative source), A | Apple HIG Widgets: "If people are likely to check your widget more frequently than you can update it, consider displaying text that describes when the data was last updated." and "show content quickly without hiding stale data behind placeholder content." |
| 2.4 | Leave the display unchanged when nothing has changed. | opinion | Guideline (single authoritative source), A | Apple HIG Live Activities: "If the underlying content or status remains the same, maintain the same display until the underlying content or status changes." |
| 2.5 | A status design system gives undefined or not-started states their own colour, and requires at least two of colour, shape and symbol. | opinion | **Single source**, B | IBM Carbon, Status indicator pattern: "Relying solely on color to convey status is insufficient"; gray to "indicate drafts or jobs that haven't been started", purple to "indicate outliers or undefined statuses". https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/status-indicator-pattern/ |
| 2.6 | Skeleton (placeholder) screens are for full-page loads of roughly 1–10 seconds. NN/g doesn't address whether placeholders can be mistaken for real values. | opinion | **Single source**, B | Tankala, "Skeleton Screens 101", NN/g, 2023-06-04: "A skeleton screen is a design pattern used to indicate that a page is loading while providing users with a wireframe-like visual that mimics the layout of the page." https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/ |
| 2.7 | Not documented in the sources opened: making stale data **visibly degrade** (fading, greying, ageing); showing unknown in gauges or number tiles specifically. Search snippets from vendor blogs (e.g. "Stale data should look stale") were not opened and make no finding. | — | not documented | — |

## 3. A screen that updates itself every minute

| # | Statement | Kind | Label / grade | Source and quote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Auto-updating information that starts on its own and sits alongside other content needs a way to pause, stop or hide it, or to control how often it updates. The exception is when the updating is essential. Level A. Whether a dashboard whose whole purpose is updating counts as "in parallel with other content" is not documented; see Q4. | fact | W3C standard, A | WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2: "For any auto-updating information that (1) starts automatically and (2) is presented in parallel with other content, there is a mechanism for the user to pause, stop, or hide it or to control the frequency of the update unless the auto-updating is part of an activity where it is essential." https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html |
| 3.2 | Content inserted above existing content causes unexpected shifts that cause wrong taps. Reserve space in advance; animate with transforms. Shifts within 500 ms of user input are excluded from the CLS measure. | fact (metric) + opinion | Guideline (single authoritative source, Google), A | web.dev "Cumulative Layout Shift": "…DOM elements are dynamically added to the page before existing content."; "A sudden shift in layout makes the user confirm a large order they intended to cancel."; "Layout shifts that occur within 500 milliseconds of user input will have the hadRecentInput flag set…"; "…create some space right away and show a loading indicator…"; "CSS transform property lets you animate elements without triggering layout shifts". https://web.dev/articles/cls |
| 3.3 | People miss changes away from where they're looking. Make one change at a time, group changing elements, use animation sparingly, and dim what didn't change. | opinion | **Single source**, B | Budiu, "Change Blindness in UX: Definition", NN/g, 2018-09-23: "Make one change at a time."; "Group all elements that will change simultaneously in the same region of the screen…"; "Use animation to signal change, but avoid having too many competing animations…"; "Dim the areas of the screen that do not change, in order to attract attention to changes." https://www.nngroup.com/articles/change-blindness-definition/ |
| 3.4 | Animate briefly, up to two seconds, to signal that new information has arrived. This conflicts with reduced-motion settings (4.4) unless it is turned off when the user asks for reduced motion. | opinion | Guideline (single authoritative source), A | Apple HIG Widgets: "use standard and custom animations with a duration of up to two seconds to let people know when new information is available or when content displays differently." |
| 3.5 | Badges or indicators can mark new or updated items. They add noise, so use them only where the information warrants it. | opinion | **Corroborated** (Flaherty, NN/g; IBM Carbon), A. Caveat: the NN/g "new or updated" point came back paraphrased; only the noise sentence is verbatim, and checker should confirm. | Flaherty, "Indicators, Validations, and Notifications", NN/g, 2024-01-17: "Indicators can introduce noise and clutter to your overall interface, and may distract users." https://www.nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications/ — Carbon: "A numbered badge is used when a count of new or updated items is available, and it's important for the user to know the exact number of updates." |
| 3.6 | Not documented: highlighting what is new **since the last visit** (as opposed to changes during a session); how long such a highlight should last. | — | not documented | — |

## 4. Standards lookup (where they differ, each is reported without choosing)

| # | Item | Source and quote | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Colour not alone | WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1 (A): "Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response, or distinguishing a visual element." https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html — Apple HIG Accessibility: "Convey information with more than color alone." https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility | A |
| 4.2 | Text contrast | **WCAG 1.4.3 (AA):** 4.5:1; large text 3:1. Large is "at least 18 point or 14 point bold". https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html — **Apple:** "Up to 17 pts / All / 4.5:1"; "18 pts / All / 3:1"; "All / Bold / 3:1". Apple gives 3:1 for bold at any size, where WCAG requires 14 pt bold. — **Google Android:** "If the text is smaller than 18sp, or if the text is bold and smaller than 14sp, … at least 4.5:1." "For all other text … at least 3:1." https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps | A each |
| 4.3 | Graphics and UI contrast | WCAG 1.4.11 (AA): 3:1 against adjacent colours for "Visual information required to identify user interface components and states" and "Parts of graphics required to understand the content". | 4.4 | Touch targets (they disagree, and the units differ: CSS px, pt, dp) | **WCAG 2.5.8 (AA):** "at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels", with exceptions for spacing, equivalent, inline, user-agent and essential. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html — **WCAG 2.5.5 (AAA):** "at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels". https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html — **Apple:** iOS/iPadOS default 44x44 pt, minimum 28x28 pt (Accessibility page table). — **Google Android:** "at least 48dpx48dp. Larger is even better." | A each |
| 4.5 | Reduced motion | WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA): "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled, unless the animation is essential…" The Understanding page gives a `prefers-reduced-motion` example. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html — Apple: "…ensure your app or game responds by reducing automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion." Material's own wording is not opened. | A |

## Proposed library entries (for the Source checker)

- **F-wcag22-01** (fact, A, topics: accessibility, mobile; shelf life 24 months): WCAG 2.2 values for 1.4.1, 1.4.3, 1.4.11, 2.5.8 (24×24, AA), 2.5.5 (44×44, AAA), 2.3.3 and 2.2.2 (auto-updating clause), as rows 4.1–4.5 and 3.1. Sources: the Understanding pages cited.
- **F-platform-targets-contrast-01** (fact, A per publisher, topics: accessibility, mobile; 12 months): Apple 44×44 pt default / 28×28 pt minimum; Apple contrast table; Android 48×48 dp and 4.5:1 / 3:1 by sp. This entry exists to record that they disagree; no choice is made.
- **P-glance-disclosure-01** (pattern, B, topics: design, dashboards, mobile; 24 months): essentials on the glance surface, detail one tap away, the tap goes straight to the matching detail, labels set expectations; more than two levels is single-source (Nielsen). Rows 1.1–1.5.
- **P-stale-visible-01** (pattern, B, topics: design, dashboards; 12 months): show last-updated time when users may look more often than the data updates; don't hide stale data behind placeholders; leave the display unchanged when nothing changed. Apple only. Rows 2.3–2.4.
- **P-zero-means-zero-01** (pattern, B, topics: design, dashboards; 24 months): "0" only for a true zero; separate marks for estimated, not available, provisional and low reliability; plotted points are read as exact unless uncertainty is shown. Rows 2.1–2.2. These are two single sources on related but different points, **not** corroborated.
- **P-live-update-layout-01** (pattern, B, topics: design, mobile; 24 months): reserve space and don't insert content above existing content; animate with transforms; one change at a time, grouped, with sparing animation. Rows 3.2–3.3.
