## The defect
Owner's ruling record: decisions/questions/P-001-card-recommendation.md ("Proceed with the fix"; "Well then we need to make sure the cards don't show up until then."; "Show it after a day, flagged (Recommended)"; then "A: own addition now (Recommended)"). The record is not in this definer's pack. This spec takes its words from the plan's quotation of it (docs/projects/P-003.md, header and Part 2).

What goes wrong today: a decision card (`dor-fail`, `stop` or `failure`) shows on the desk as soon as it is in queue/, and its Recommended mark comes only from its own section 5. The Chief of Staff's recommendation is in a separate notes file, `decisions/<item>/<gate>-<n>-notes.md`, which the desk never reads (plan §2). This happened twice: decisions/Q-019/stop-1.md and decisions/Q-017/stop-2.md were answered `re-specify` where the notes recommended `drop` (plan §2).

## Acceptance criteria (scenarios; owner-approved, decisions/P-003/criteria-9.md, "proceed")
Every criterion is a plain scenario traced to V6: *decision cards shown to the owner with neither the Chief of Staff's recommended button marked nor the "No Chief of Staff recommendation yet" mark, 0, from the day M4 is live to 2026-12-31*. A V6 miss is also any decision card held off the queue more than a day after it was raised. A card not shown because a read could not get the records is not a miss (plan §6).

"The records" means queue/, the notes files and the dispatch log as they stand on the main branch. Nothing else decides a decision card's state or which word it shows: no memory of earlier reads, nothing kept on a device. A **read** is the desk's or the sender's reading of those records. A card's **age** at a moment is that moment minus its raise time as recorded in its `card` entry, never reset or adjusted. "Raised less than a day ago" means an age under 24 hours, including a negative age (J1). "A day ago or more" means an age of 24 hours or more.

A **baseline run** is a run of the desk's code, or of the notification sender, as it stands at the build's **base commit** (the main-branch commit the build starts from, before any change for this fix), on inputs a test sets. Its outputs are recorded in a file under tests/desk/ that names the base commit, committed on the build branch before any change under src/. "As today" and "as before this fix" (AC9, AC14 to AC17) mean equal to that recorded output for the same inputs, and the criterion's test compares against it.

### Holding and showing (the rule, D1, and what the owner sees, D2)
- **AC1** [trace: V6 → AC1] — When a decision card is in queue/, its notes are not on the main branch and it was raised less than a day ago, then the desk holds it. It is not on the owner's queue, not in Home's count of what needs the owner and not on the board. No view offers its answer buttons: a direct link or a notice to it opens what the desk opens today for a card not on its list (AC15). No notification is sent for it.
- **AC2** [trace: V6 → AC2] — When a held decision card's notes reach the main branch with a recommended word, then on the open desk's next read the card shows on the queue, in Home's count and on the board. Its card sheet says the Chief of Staff recommends that word, and that word's button, and only that one, is marked Recommended. Its notification is sent at the sender's next run (AC14).
- **AC3** [trace: V6 → AC3] — When a decision card's card sheet shows, then its own section 5 marks no button, whatever word it names. On a decision card only the notes' recommended word marks a button. (Gate cards keep today's behaviour, AC9.)
- **AC4** [trace: V6 → AC4] — When a decision card was raised a day (24 hours) ago or more by its `card` entry and still has no notes on the main branch, then it shows on the queue, in Home's count and on the board. Its card sheet carries the mark "No Chief of Staff recommendation yet", and no button is marked Recommended. Its notification is sent at the sender's next run (AC14). If the desk is open as the day passes, the card shows on the desk's next read, without a reload.
- **AC5** [trace: V6 → AC5] — When a card shown with the "No Chief of Staff recommendation yet" mark later gets notes with a recommended word on the main branch, then on the desk's next read the mark is gone and the card shows the notes' word with that word's button marked Recommended, as in AC2. For its notification, AC14 applies.
- **AC6** [trace: V6 → AC6] — When a decision card's notes are on the main branch but have no recommended word (no `Recommendation:` line as J2 defines it, no backticked word on that line, or no backticked word on it that is one of the card's words), then the desk treats the card as having no notes. It is held under AC1 within its first day, shown with the "No Chief of Staff recommendation yet" mark under AC4 after that, and never shown with a button marked.
- **AC7** [trace: V6 → AC7] — When a read gets the dispatch log and a decision card's raise time is older than the part of the log the desk's existing log reader reads, then the card shows at once. It shows as in AC4, with the "No Chief of Staff recommendation yet" mark, or as in AC2 if its notes with a recommended word are on the main branch. A new card's `card` entry reaches the main branch with its card file (decisions/P-003/stop-7.md), so a card in queue/ whose entry is not in the part of the log that was read is a card raised before that part. The rule takes a missing entry, in a log it got, as this case and no other. (A read that did not get the log at all is not this case: AC17.)
- **AC8** [trace: V6 → AC8] — When notes for a decision card exist only on a branch other than the main branch (for example in an open pull request), then the desk treats the card as having no notes.

### Unchanged
- **AC9** [trace: V6 → AC9] — When a card is a gate card (`dor`, `criteria` or `launch`), an owner question or a pull-request card, then it shows, counts and notifies exactly as before this fix, with its Recommended mark from its gate case as today, and the desk does not look for notes for it. A test runs the desk's card-making code and the sender on sample records holding each of these kinds: once with every read getting the records, then with each of the three reads failing in turn (queue/, a notes file, the dispatch log). It checks that what they give for these cards (on the list or not, Home's count, the marked button, any line shown, any notice) equals a baseline run on the same records and failures.
- **AC10** [trace: V6 → AC10] — When the desk reads notes, then it reads them on the read path it already uses, from the repositories it already reads, with the access it already has and on the schedule it already keeps. Whether a notes file exists comes from the file tree the desk already reads. The one new kind of read is a notes file's content: one read for each decision card in queue/ whose notes file is in that tree (plan §1 Out, "The desk reads one new kind of record"). The desk writes nothing to any repository, uses no new credential and sends nothing to any new outside service; its spend stays $0 (plan §5). A read of this new kind does not breach this criterion. A read of a repository the desk does not already read, a new credential, a write, or a call to a new service does.

### The views and the sender agree, from the records alone
- **AC11** [trace: V6 → AC11] — When the queue, Home's count, the board, the card sheet (including the hand-written one in src/public/sandbox.js) and the notification sender are given the same records and the same moment, then each decision card has the same state in all of them: held, shown with a recommended word (and the same word), or shown with the "No Chief of Staff recommendation yet" mark. Each takes the card list the one rule makes from those records and that moment, and from nothing else. The same records and moment always give the same state, whatever was read before, so a desk open for hours, a desk just opened and the sender all agree. A read that did not get some of the records gives no state to the decision cards it cannot decide (AC16, AC17). To the ones it can decide (AC17) it gives the state a full read gives, never a different one. (Whether a notice is sent is not part of a card's state; AC14 and AC15 cover it.) The rule never reads a clock; the moment is always handed to it, since the desk and the sender never run at the same instant. A test hands the views' card-making path and the sender's the same sample records and one injected moment, at several moments (before, at and after a card's raise time plus 24 hours), and checks that every decision card's state is the same in all of them.

### After graduation: running and monitoring
*The desk has graduated (docs/PROJECT.md:127, Launch-5). The weekly V6 count, its reports of misses, the questions that carry the owner's feedback and the review card after 2026-12-31 are the Chief of Staff's (plan §4, §6), and by the owner's ruling (decisions/P-003/stop-5.md) they are not built in this spec. This section, with AC18 and AC19, covers what the desk's code and the Builder's handover notes must give so that those can be run.*
- **AC12** [trace: V6 → AC12] — Running: when M4 is live, then it needs no new setting, credential, schedule or step from the owner or any agent. The notes reads of AC10 run on the desk's existing reads and schedule, so they start and stop with them.
- **AC13** [trace: V6 → AC13] — Monitoring (the plan's "monitoring criterion", §6): when anyone runs the one rule on the records as they stood on the main branch at a past commit, at a moment they choose, then it gives, for each decision card in queue/ at that commit, its id, its raise time as recorded (or "not recorded") and the state the rule gives it at that moment. If those records cannot be got, it gives the AC16 or AC17 flag instead. It reads nothing else: no clock of its own, no network, nothing stored. It also runs on records from before M4 was live, so the Chief of Staff can run the count once on past records before the Launch gate (plan §6). Run at the check moments J6 names, it gives V6's held-too-long count and, beside it, a count of cards held a day after they reached the main branch. J6 checks each card at every moment from its due time onward at which its state can change, so a card that is `held` at any of those moments is found. A test runs the rule at J6's check moments on sample records holding two cards, works out J6's two figures from the states the rule gives, and checks them:
  - (a) a card whose `card` entry, reaching the main branch with its card file, records a raise time a day later than that, from clock skew: reported as held a day after reaching the main branch, and not a V6 miss;
  - (b) a card raised and shown on time: neither figure reports it.
  The test also checks the ids, raise times and states the rule gives, including for a card raised before the part of the log that is read (reported `not recorded`, shown).

  What these counts can and cannot see. By the plan's meaning of "raised" (§8, the card's `card` entry), the V6 held-too-long figure can be other than 0 only if the rule's code on the main branch, at some commit after M4, holds a card past its recorded day. The rule as M4 builds it never does that (AC4, AC7), so that figure guards against a later change to the rule. The second figure finds holds that a skewed or malformed raise time causes (case (a)). By the plan's words it is not part of V6 (open question 2). Neither count finds V6's first half, a card shown with neither mark. Every shown state the rule gives carries one of the two marks, so a view that drops the mark, or shows a card the rule did not list, leaves no sign in the records. That half is guarded by the tests of J4 and AC11 before the fix goes live, not measured after it (Risks; open question 4).

### Notifications
- **AC14** [trace: V6 → AC14] — When the notification sender runs, then it takes the card list the one rule makes and sends for decision cards as it sends today for any card on that list, with no change to its own files (src/notify/**). It sends no notice for a held card (AC1) or for a card not shown because a read could not get the records (AC16, AC17). It sends a notice at the first run at which the card is shown (AC2, AC4, AC7, AC17). Two cases keep today's sender behaviour unchanged:
  - A shown card that changes from `shown, no recommendation yet` to `shown, recommended <word>` (AC5) is treated as today's sender treats a card that stays on its list with changed content.
  - A card that leaves the list (back to held, J3, or not shown on a failed read, AC16, AC17) and later shows again is treated as today's sender treats a card that leaves its list and comes back.

  A test runs the sender over a chosen sequence of records and moments, including both cases. It checks that no notice is sent while a card is held or not shown, and one is sent at first showing. For the two cases, it checks that the notices equal a baseline run of the sender (src/notify/** at the base commit) handed the same sequence of card lists at the point where it takes the list from the card-making code (J5). If the base commit's sender cannot be handed lists at that point as it stands, the baseline run may give the card-making module the sender imports a test stand-in under tests/desk/ that returns those lists, with no change to any file under src/notify/**. If neither can be done, the Builder stops and returns, as J5 says. This criterion holds whatever way today's sender avoids repeating a notice, since it is checked against the recorded run.
- **AC15** [trace: V6 → AC15] — When a notice has been sent for a decision card and the card then leaves the list (back to held, J3, or not shown on a failed read, AC16, AC17), then this fix withdraws nothing and the sender sends nothing for the card while it is off the list. While it is off the list, opening that notice, or a direct link to the card, opens what the desk opens today for a card not on its list: no answer buttons, and no new card-sheet state. Once the card shows again, the notice or link opens it as shown, as for any card on the list. "What the desk opens today" is checked against a baseline run: a test opens a notice and a direct link for a card id that is not on the list at the base commit and records what the desk shows. After the fix, a held or not-shown decision card's notice and link must show the same.

### When a read cannot get the records (plan §1, fifth and sixth bullets)
- **AC16** [trace: V6 → AC16] — When a read of the desk or of the sender cannot get queue/, or cannot get a notes file the rule needs (one that is on the main branch for a decision card in queue/), then from that read no decision card is shown on the queue, counted in Home, put on the board or offered with answer buttons, and the sender sends no notice for any decision card on that run. Instead the queue and Home show the line "Decision cards not shown: queue/ could not be read" or "Decision cards not shown: the Chief of Staff's notes could not be read", or both lines if both reads failed. When a later read gets the records, each card is held or shown by AC1 to AC8 and AC17, with its real age. Gate cards, questions and pull-request cards behave as today when a read fails (AC9): what the desk and the sender give for them equals a baseline run with the same records and the same read failing.
- **AC17** [trace: V6 → AC17] — When a read gets queue/ and the notes but cannot get the dispatch log, then each decision card whose notes with a recommended word are on the main branch is shown, counted in Home, put on the board, offered with answer buttons and notified exactly as in AC2, as when the log is read. No other decision card in queue/ is shown, counted, put on the board or offered with answer buttons from that read, and the sender sends no notice for any of them on that run. While at least one decision card is left out for this reason, the queue and Home show the line "Decision cards without Chief of Staff notes not shown: the dispatch log could not be read". When a later read gets the log, each card is held or shown by AC1 to AC8 with its real age. Gate cards, questions and pull-request cards behave as today (AC9): what the desk and the sender give for them equals a baseline run with the same records and the log read failing.

## Join sheets
- **J1 —** queue/ and the dispatch log on the main branch → the rule (D1): for each card file `queue/<item>-<gate>-<n>.md` (for example `queue/Q-003-failure-1.md`; the form is inferred, see Known limits), its kind comes from `<gate>`, and its raise time from its `card` entry in the dispatch log the desk already reads (plan §7). It is a decision card if `<gate>` is `dor-fail`, `stop` or `failure`, and a gate card if `<gate>` is `dor`, `criteria` or `launch`. The field names of a `card` entry are those of the desk's existing log reader in src/lib/ (plan §7); the Builder states them in the J1 test. Awkward cases:
  - A `dor-fail` card must not be read as a `dor` gate card by matching the prefix `dor`. It is a decision card.
  - The log is got, but the part the reader reads has no `card` entry for the card. This means the card was raised before that part (AC7), so it is shown at once.
  - The read does not get the log: the card is shown only if its notes with a recommended word are on the main branch (AC17).
  - queue/ is not got: no decision card gets a state (AC16).
  - Two or more `card` entries for one card. The rule itself makes the pick, in src/lib/: the earliest raise time among the entries the existing log reader returns for the card, whatever order they come in. If the reader returns only one of them, that one is the raise time. AC13 and J6 report the same pick, so the desk, the sender and the count agree. The J1 test includes a log with two entries for one card and states which of them the reader returns. The plan does not settle this pick (open question 6).
  - A recorded raise time later than the moment of the read, from clock skew. Age is always measured from the recorded time and never reset, so a negative age counts as less than a day: the card is held until the recorded time plus 24 hours, then shown. For example, with a raise time recorded as 2026-11-10 10:00, a read at 09:58 that day → held; a read at 2026-11-11 09:59 → held; a read at 2026-11-11 10:00 → shown.
  - A raise time far in the future, from a malformed entry, follows the same rule: the card is held until that time plus 24 hours (Risks).
  A test marked `join-test: S-006/J1` covers it.
- **J2 —** the main branch's file tree and a notes file → the rule (D1): whether `decisions/<item>/<gate>-<n>-notes.md` exists on the main branch for card `queue/<item>-<gate>-<n>.md`, and if it does, its recommended word. To find the notes' `Recommendation:` line, take each line of the file in order, strip any heading marks (`#`, then spaces) and any bold or italic marks (`**`, `*`, `_`) from its start, and take the first line that then begins with `Recommendation:`. A line that only mentions `Recommendation:` later in its text is not it. The recommended word is the first backticked word on that line that is one of the card's words (the words its buttons offer), matched exactly. Examples, for a card whose words are `drop`, `re-specify` and `resubmit`:
  - `Recommendation: \`drop\`` → `drop`
  - `**Recommendation:** \`drop\` — the scope is gone` → `drop`
  - `_Recommendation:_ \`resubmit\`` → `resubmit`
  - `## Recommendation: \`re-specify\`` → `re-specify`
  - `Recommendation: not \`proceed\`; \`drop\`` → `drop` (`proceed` is not one of the card's words)
  - `Recommendation: \`Drop\`` → none (not an exact match), so no notes (AC6)
  - `Recommendation: drop` (no backticks) → none, so no notes (AC6)
  - an earlier prose line `The Recommendation: line below says \`resubmit\``, then `Recommendation: \`drop\`` → `drop` (the prose line does not begin with `Recommendation:`)
  - no line beginning with `Recommendation:` → none, so no notes (AC6)
  - two lines beginning with `Recommendation:` → the first decides; if it has no recommended word, the result is none (the second is not read)
  - notes for `stop-2` beside a card `stop-1` → not this card's notes
  - a notes file listed in the tree whose content the read could not get → this is not "no notes": the read has not got the notes (AC16)
  A test marked `join-test: S-006/J2` covers it.
- **J3 —** the rule (D1) → the one card list every view reads. Each decision card the read can decide carries one state, `held`, `shown, recommended <word>` or `shown, no recommendation yet`, worked out from the records and the moment alone (AC11). Nothing about earlier reads is carried.
  - A read that did not get queue/ or the notes gives the list no decision cards, and the flag `decision cards not read: <queue/ | notes>` (either or both, AC16).
  - A read that got queue/ and the notes but not the dispatch log gives the list only the decision cards whose notes with a recommended word are on the main branch, each `shown, recommended <word>`. If any decision card was left out, it adds the flag `decision cards without notes not read: dispatch log` (AC17).
  - Gate cards, questions and pull-request cards carry no new state.
  Awkward cases:
  - A card answered while held leaves the list, as any answered card does.
  - Notes arriving in the same read as the day passing: the notes win (AC2).
  - Notes removed from the main branch after a card showed with their word: the state is worked out again. It is `shown, no recommendation yet` if the card was raised a day ago or more or before the part of the log that is read, and `held` if it was raised less than a day ago.
  The last case is the only way a shown card goes back to held. A card's `card` entry lands with its card file (AC7), so a card is never shown for a missing entry and then held when the entry arrives. A failed read (AC16, AC17) takes a card off the list without giving it a state. A test marked `join-test: S-006/J3` covers it.
- **J4 —** the card list → the views (the queue, Home's count, the board, and the card sheet in src/public/sandbox.js and its generated copies):
  - `held` cards appear in none of them, and no view offers their answer buttons.
  - `shown, recommended <word>` cards show the word in the card sheet and mark that button Recommended.
  - `shown, no recommendation yet` cards show the mark and mark no button.
  - The `decision cards not read` flag shows its line or lines on the queue and on Home (AC16), and the `decision cards without notes not read` flag shows its line there (AC17), each with the exact words those criteria give.
  Awkward cases:
  - A card sheet opened from a direct link to a held card, or from a notice sent before the card left the list, opens what the desk opens today for a card not on its list (AC15).
  - A card sheet left open while the card moves from `shown, no recommendation yet` to `shown, recommended <word>`: the mark gives way on the next read.
  - A card sheet left open when a read fails shows what the desk shows today for a card not on its list (AC15).
  A test marked `join-test: S-006/J4` covers it.
- **J5 —** the card list → the notification sender. The sender takes the same card list (src/notify/collect.mjs already runs the desk's card-making code outside the browser, docs/PROJECT.md:588), so a held decision card is not on the list it notifies from, and a shown one is. What is handed over: the card list at that run, with each card's id, kind and state, plus the flags of AC16 and AC17 if the read did not get the records. Nothing in the sender's own files changes; its existing way of not repeating a notice applies to decision cards as to any card (AC14). Awkward cases:
  - The sender's read does not get queue/ or the notes: no notice for any decision card on that run; gate cards as today (AC16).
  - The sender's read does not get the log: notices as usual for cards whose notes with a recommended word are on the main branch, and none for the rest (AC17).
  - A card shows and is answered between two sender runs: no notification is owed.
  - A notice was already sent for a card that leaves the list: nothing is withdrawn, and nothing is sent while the card is off the list (AC15).
  If meeting AC1, AC2, AC14, AC16 or AC17 turns out to need a change to src/notify/**, or AC14's baseline run cannot be made without one, the build stops and comes back to the owner (plan §4). The spec is then revised, not built around. A test marked `join-test: S-006/J5` covers it.
- **J6 —** the rule (D1) → the Chief of Staff's V6 count (AC13). What is handed over, for one commit of the main branch and one moment: a list with one entry per decision card in queue/ at that commit, giving its id (`<item>-<gate>-<n>`), its raise time as recorded in the dispatch log (ISO 8601, or `not recorded`) and its state (`held`, `shown, recommended <word>` or `shown, no recommendation yet`). If the records at that commit cannot be got, the list carries the AC16 or AC17 flag instead, as on the desk.

  How the count uses it is set down in the operating notes (AC18). These terms are all taken from the main branch's history:
  - **The main line** is the main branch's first-parent history, so a commit made on a pull-request branch counts from when it reached the main branch.
  - **The commit current at a moment** is the latest commit on the main line whose committer time is at or before that moment.
  - **A card's arrival** is the committer time of the first commit on the main line whose tree holds its card file.
  - **A card's check moments, from a due time**, are the due time itself plus the committer time of each later commit on the main line that changes queue/, any notes file, the dispatch log or the rule's code in src/lib/. They stop at the first commit whose tree no longer holds the card file. Between two such commits only time passes, and the passing of time can turn a held card to shown but never the reverse (J3). So a card that is `held` at any moment from the due time on is `held` at one of these moments.
  - At each check moment the rule is run on the records at the commit current then, with the rule's code as it stood at that commit. For a commit before M4 was merged, the code as M4 merged it is used.
  The figures:
  - **V6 held-too-long miss** (plan §6, with "raised" as plan §8 defines it). The due time is the recorded raise time plus 24 hours. A card is a miss if it is `held` at any of its check moments. A card with no recorded raise time is never `held` (AC7).
  - **Held a day after reaching the main branch**, reported with V6 but not counted in it (open question 2). The due time is the card's arrival plus 24 hours. The card is reported if it is `held` at any of its check moments.
  - **No-mark miss**: a card shown with neither mark. Every shown state the rule gives carries one of the two marks, so a count from the rule never finds one. A view that shows a card off the list, or drops its mark, is not seen by this count (AC13, Risks, open question 4).
  Worked examples:
  - A card file and its `card` entry arrive together at 2026-11-10 10:00, but the entry records 2026-11-11 09:00, from skew. There are no notes. For V6, the due time is 2026-11-12 09:00, when the age is 24 hours, so the card is `shown, no recommendation yet`. No later commit holds it, so it is not a miss. For the second figure, the due time is 2026-11-11 10:00, when the age is 1 hour, so the card is `held` and is reported.
  - A card raised and arriving at 2026-11-10 10:00, with no notes. At 2026-11-11 10:00 it is `shown, no recommendation yet`, so neither figure has a miss. If a commit on 2026-11-14 changed src/lib/ so that the rule held the card, it would be `held` at that commit's time, which is a V6 miss.
  Awkward cases:
  - A card answered before its due time: its card file has left queue/, so it has no check moments and is not a miss.
  - A check moment at which the records cannot be got: the list carries the AC16 or AC17 flag. That is a failed read, not a miss (plan §6), and it is reported as a failed read.
  - A raise time far in the future: never a V6 miss while the rule is unchanged, since the card is shown at its due time. The second figure reports it once a day has passed since the card's arrival.
  - A card raised before the part of the log that is read: shown at once, so neither figure reports it.
  - Two `card` entries: the rule's own pick (J1), the same as the desk's.
  - A committer time that is wrong, or a deploy that lags the main branch: see Risks.
  A test marked `join-test: S-006/J6` covers the handed-over list's form, the first worked example, and case (b) of AC13 at their check moments. It may be the same test as AC13's.

## Risks and mitigations
- A card held for good. AC4 (a day) and AC7 (raised before the log that is read: shown at once) prevent this. J1 measures age from the recorded raise time and never resets it, so clock skew delays a card only by the amount of the skew. A malformed raise time far in the future would hold a card until that time plus a day. By the plan's meaning of "raised" that is not a V6 miss, but J6's second figure finds it a day after the card reaches the main branch. Accepted; open question 2.
- The count finding nothing. While the rule is the one M4 merged, the V6 held-too-long figure is 0 by construction; what each figure can and cannot find is said openly in AC13 and AC18.
- A card hidden while a record cannot be read. AC16 and AC17 put a line on the queue and Home naming what could not be read. Cards with a recommendation still show while only the log is missing, and the hidden cards come back on the first read that gets the records. This is not a V6 miss (plan §6), so the line is the only sign of a long outage; AC18 explains it.
- A card missing from the board and from notices, with no sign there, while a read fails. The owner would hate "a card silently missing" (plan, Intent). Home and the queue, where the owner looks for what needs them, carry the line. Rated non-blocking in the join-up review and carried into the Build gate's case. Open question 5.
- One notes file that cannot be fetched hides every decision card (AC16), as the plan's "cannot get ... the notes" reads. This is wider than needed, but it keeps one rule, and the line says so. Open question 3.
- A button marked that the Chief of Staff did not recommend. Exact matching (J2, AC6) means anything unclear counts as no notes. J2's line rule stops a prose mention from deciding the word, and AC3 stops section 5 from marking a decision card.
- The owner told twice. No notice is sent while a card is held or not shown. For a word that arrives later, or a card that shows again, the sender behaves as today (AC14); open question 1.
- A notice for a card that then leaves the list, opening to what the desk shows for a card not on its list. This happens in only two cases (J3): notes are removed from the main branch within a card's first day, or a read fails. It cannot happen from a `card` entry arriving after its card, because the entry lands with the card file (decisions/P-003/stop-7.md). In both cases the card shows again by the rules. Accepted, AC15.
- A gate card changed by mistake. AC9 and J1's `dor-fail` / `dor` case guard against it.
- V6's first half is not measured on the live desk. The count cannot see a view that drops the mark or shows a card the rule did not list. J4's and AC11's tests check every view against the rule before launch, and AC18 says what the counts cover. This is carried to the Launch gate as a risk; open question 4.
- J6 trusts committer times and reads the main branch's code, not the live deployment. A wrong commit clock shifts the second figure by the error. A deploy that lags the main branch, or a rollback, means the live desk may run different code from the code J6 reads. The live deploy is the owner's ruling, so the Chief of Staff knows when it lags.
- A baseline recorded from the wrong code. The baseline file names the base commit and is committed before any change under src/, so the Reviewer can check both on the branch.
- The build going past the stop rule. The test estimate is near it. The Builder reports lines and tests against the stop rule, which applies unchanged.
- The owner relying on the mark without reading the reasons: automation bias "cannot be prevented by training or instructions" (library/facts/F-automation-complacency-01.md:4, B). The card sheet still shows the card in full, and the mark only names the Chief of Staff's word. Accepted, since the owner asked for the mark.
