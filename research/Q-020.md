# Research question — Q-020

*Drafted by the Chief of Staff; checked by the Critic for gaps and neutral phrasing (D-032, D-055).*

## The question

What does published evidence show on how one decision-maker is kept informed about many changes or events, and where such briefing has misled, been skipped, or added little?

The groundwork map found no source it opened that covers this (research/Q-016-memo.md, area P). Ways of keeping one reader informed that are in view include: reading each item as it arrives; a list or status page; a periodic digest; alerts only; summaries written by the author, by another person or by a machine; and leaving things as they are. Nothing in this question assumes that any of these works. Errors in machine-written summaries are a separate question (Q-021).

From published studies, practitioner research and well-documented practice (for example, change logs and release notes, executive, intelligence or military briefings, project status reports, and incident and change-management practice):
- what helps one reader keep track of many changes (seen one at a time, grouped, in relation to each other, or only as exceptions), including what is known from the oversight of automated systems, and what has been found to mislead, to be skipped, to cause items to be missed, or to add little;
- what is known about alert fatigue and the volume of notices a reader acts on, given volumes that swing several-fold between weeks (research/Q-016-memo.md, area D);
- whether status reports or summaries written by the party who did the work have been found to differ in accuracy or optimism from those written by someone else;
- what is known about how much a reader takes in at once on a phone screen, for this kind of content;
- for each finding, how strong the evidence is and how close its setting is to one owner overseeing several projects built mostly by AI agents.

Paywalled studies count only where an abstract or an open version can be read; say which was read. Grade vendor claims as vendor claims.

## The decision it serves

The design traps and the shape of any way of keeping the owner informed, and whether outside evidence supports building anything beyond what exists.

## What each answer would change

Evidence that a form works, or fails, in a close setting makes it a candidate or a trap; weak or distant evidence leaves the choice open or to a cheap trial.
