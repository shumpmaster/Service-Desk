# Ruling needed: The widget test couldn't reach what it was meant to check. What next?

Milestone: none (the widget's milestone, M5, is still only a proposal in its plan, so nothing here serves a milestone yet)
Asked in session: session_01QBwHGJ3UVJ6HY4SS92w7UW

WHY: The widget test ran on your phone for a day and came back "not judged": neither a pass nor a fail. Inside the Scriptable app, the first full read finished, and a later refresh matched the desk's own summary exactly. But on the home screen, all 25 of the widget's own refreshes were stopped by the phone within seconds, and none of them started from the copy the app had kept. The likely reason is that the home-screen widget can't see the folder where the app kept that copy (Caches), while it can see the folder that holds the log (Documents). So the step the test was built to measure, a home-screen refresh starting from a kept copy, never happened. The test's written rules don't cleanly cover a case where that step never happens at all: they say to run the test once more after a "not judged", but they define "not judged" only for a counted refresh that did run. The Reviewer chose "not judged" as the nearest outcome and left the next step to you. Run unchanged, the test should hit the same wall. Until you rule, no more work is done on the widget. Nothing the desk does or shows changes, whichever you choose.
OPTIONS:

Documents is copied by your phone's iCloud backup, and your Phone only ruling didn't settle whether the backup is allowed; that stays for the Design gate. The test data comes back by the route you allowed last time, which this extends to the second test. Your time: about 15 minutes to set up, a day of normal use, about 10 minutes at the end. This test stays not judged. The widget may still be stopped for time or memory; a fail or another not-judged result comes back to you, with the two fallbacks below."
  B. "Sender saves a summary" — "You treat the widget route as failed, though the test didn't fail it. P-004 takes the first fallback you were offered: the desk's notification sender also saves a summary, and the widget only reads that. It changes the sender, which needs your approval, and where the summary is saved has to keep your private projects out of the desk's public repository. Not sized yet."
  C. "Counts-only widget" — "You treat the widget route as failed, though the test didn't fail it. P-004 takes the other fallback: a widget that reads a few counts straight from GitHub and doesn't show the desk's own summary. No desk change; the lightest to build.   D. "Repeat the test as it is" — "The Builder rebuilds the same test widget (this session's copy is gone) and you run it once more, the step the test's rules give after a not-judged result, though they don't cleanly cover this case. Another day of your phone and about 25 minutes of your time: 15 to install the rebuilt widget and its key, and 10 at the end. Expect the same result, because the widget still can't see the kept copy; if it is not judged again, this question comes back."
RECOMMENDATION: A, because it is the only option that can still tell whether the widget you chose can work, it costs a day of your phone and about half an hour of your time, and both fallbacks stay open if it fails. My confidence is medium: the widget may also be stopped for time or memory even with the kept copy, and nothing measured so far rules that out.
