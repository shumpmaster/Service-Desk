## 1. Scope
*What is in, what is explicitly out, and where it must work (target devices or places).*

An **event** is one recorded step of the agent team's work: a GitHub object (a branch, commit, pull request, check, review, merge or deploy), or a step of the operating model that has no GitHub counterpart (a phase gate, a decision, research, a stop). The **timeline** is the desk's view of every event across the owner's projects, drawn like a CI run: kinds by shape, states by colour and by word, who by name, as the basis sets out. Section 8 defines the other terms.

In, as the owner's answers set it (decisions/questions/P-005-groundwork-shortlist.md):
- One timeline across all projects by default, which condenses and expands by the projects in focus or with something that needs the owner (answer 5).
- What waits on the owner comes first, shown in the context of the branches, pull requests and commits around it (answer 1).
- The owner acts from the timeline: each answer, approval and merge starts from the timeline and finishes with one tap on GitHub's own page, as the owner's own GitHub action (answer 4; the owner's ruling "A: Finish on GitHub (Recommended)", decisions/questions/P-005-acting.md). The desk holds no key that can write; acting inside the timeline is a later change by its own ruling.
- Any single thing that needs the owner notifies their phone (answer 2).

- The coding work, the series of pull requests, research questions, sources, approvals and design mockups, all captured in the timeline, shown in it or opened from its events (answer 8).

*In plain language: the chain from the deliverables to the value — how this work produces it.*

## 6. Value and stop rule
*This project's own measure of value, the target and date, and what counts as a miss — including the cost to the owner's time. Write each value target on its own line starting with its ID, like `V1: <measure>, <target>, <date>`; spec criteria trace to these IDs.*

*Draft targets, misses, costs and stop rule, for the owner to approve, change or replace at the Design gate; never set by proxy.*

V7: Owner wait: the median time a step waits on the owner, from when the desk's rules show it to the owner's action on it, or the Chief of Staff's as the owner's proxy (D-066), listed apart, at most half its baseline measured by experiment E1 by the same rule, over the first 30 days after launch
V8: Unseen steps: steps waiting on the owner that were neither sent to the phone nor shown on the timeline while it was open within 5 minutes of the time the desk's rules make them due, 0 over the first 30 days after launch
V9: Faithful: events shown wrongly in a weekly hand check against the records, 0 over the first 30 days after launch

V7 to V9 are numbered after the desk's V1 to V6 so that no two of the desk's targets share a name.

How they are measured:
- V7: a wait starts and ends at these record times: a decision card, when P-003's rule shows it (computed by rerunning that rule on the dispatch log and the notes, since no record holds it), to its answer commit; a question, its file's pull request opened, to its answer record; a gate, its case filed, to the ruling; a pull request that needs the owner, ready, to merged; a deploy, requested, to approved. Steps that start waiting within the 30 days count; one still open at the end counts at its wait so far, as V5 does. Whether a proxy action counts is put to the owner at the Design gate. E1 measures the baseline by exactly this rule.
- V8: counted from the notification sender's run logs on GitHub Actions (kept 90 days by default, which covers the 30) and a log of when the timeline was open, kept on the owner's device and committed weekly from a prefilled link, as V1's was; the timeline keeps that log and link (D3). A step not shown because the view or its feed had stopped and said so is counted separately, not as a miss. The access research found that GitHub's schedules run at most every 5 minutes and can be delayed or dropped, and that webhooks can take minutes and are not redelivered automatically (F-gh-12, F-gh-12b, F-Q004-3a, F-Q004-1; research/Q-027-memo.md), so the 5-minute figure goes to the owner at the Design gate with that risk.
- V9: each week a reviewer who did not build the timeline checks a sample (20 events and every step that waited on the owner) against the records by hand, and counts any wrong kind, state, who or time, any event no record supports, any reconstructed step not marked as one, and any "not recorded" field shown with a value.

A miss: V7 above half its baseline; any V8 or V9 count above 0. The cost to the owner's time: none for V7 and V9; V8's device log costs the owner one commit a week from a prefilled link. The Chief of Staff runs the checks and reports each miss to the owner the same week.

Stop rule: work stops and comes back to the owner before anything more is done if experiment E2 shows that a step which waits on the owner, or any of the key's eleven kinds, has no record giving its time and who, so V7, V8 or V9 could not be measured; if the access research shows acting from the timeline needs spend or a credential the owner has not approved, in which case the owner rules whether acting drops out; if E1's baseline has fewer than 10 waits in its 30 days or a median under 1 hour (both drafted here for the owner); if Q-024 points to an alert limit that conflicts with answer 2; if the build's size exceeds the ceiling set in Design once the work is sized; or if a third readiness or plan-review round fails.

*Every load-bearing unknown, each answered with evidence (library entry or research memo), including through research sub-projects if needed. Name the groundwork map this rests on (research/<Q-id>-memo.md) and the owner's answers to its shortlist, or say why no map was needed.*

Load-bearing unknowns, each with what answers it. Every research memo below passed its source check, and the entries named are filed in library/:
- What an event record must hold for work to be rebuilt from records alone: answered by Q-023 (research/Q-023-memo.md). A record names who acted, on whose behalf and in what role (F-rec-audit-content-01, F-rec-prov-delegation-01, F-rec-prov-attribution-association-01); GitHub's `actor` and `triggering_actor` can differ, and a re-run counts as a new attempt (F-rec-actions-actor-attempt-01); each event has its own id and links to what it follows from (F-rec-event-identity-01, P-rec-correlation-causation-01); when a thing happened is kept apart from when it was recorded (F-rec-two-times-01). The project, piece of work and milestone come from the model's own records. Whether this setup's records hold all of it: experiment E2, open.
- How long GitHub keeps its records: answered by Q-023 and Q-027. Checks, workflow runs and statuses expire with the Actions retention setting, 90 days by default (F-gh-retention-01, F-rec-actions-retention-02); that this took effect on 2026-10-01 rests on one GitHub changelog page, while GitHub's docs pages still say it will apply (F-gh-retention-02), and all three entries are GitHub's own; so a fact the timeline needs from them is copied into a lasting record when it happens (P-rec-self-contained-durable-01). Where that copy is kept is open (below).
- What interrupts the owner and what waits, and what overload and ignored displays teach: answered by Q-024 (research/Q-024-memo.md). No source gives a daily alert limit for one person who is not watching all the time; the nearest, about two pages per twelve-hour on-call shift, is not a finding for this setting (F-oncall-ceiling-01). So answer 2 stands, since nothing sets a limit for this setting. The rest is weaker and shapes the design only: in one clinical self-report survey, far from this setting, alert volume did not predict missed items while feeling overloaded did (F-alert-volume-vs-miss-01); grouping alerts (F-alertmanager-grouping-01, single page) and repeating one only when it changes (P-repeat-on-change-01, untested for overseeing AI agents) are the patterns found. Whether an iPhone web-app alert can be marked Time Sensitive is unknown (F-ios-interruption-levels-01), and stays open.
- How the view shows its as-of time, sources and gaps, and how a stopped feed is noticed: answered by Q-025 (research/Q-025-memo.md), in section 1's rules (F-asof-age-label-01, P-feed-deadman-01, F-log-gap-reporting-01, F-provenance-self-generated-01, F-agent-false-success-01).
- How events are classified, including the model's own steps: answered by Q-026 (research/Q-026-memo.md). Six of twelve sampled systems, by the Researcher's grouping, layer a small closed set, used for counting and colour, over an open kind named by whoever produces the event, plus free text; two arguably do and four do not (P-event-vocab-layered-01); unbounded values are never used as categories (F-high-cardinality-01), and closed sets carry "unknown" and "other" values (F-enum-escape-values-01). The model's own steps go in the same record form under a name whose owner is stated, or in a stream of their own (F-cloudevents-type-owner-01, F-change-events-separate-01); showing both on one timeline is the memo's reading, not a filed fact. People handle about six to seven colour categories well, in studies of scatterplots; applying that to a timeline is an inference (F-category-count-limit-01), so the key's eleven kinds are shown by shape, and colour is kept for states.

Acting is set by the owner's ruling (section 1). Reading with today's read-only key cannot see GitHub's check results; a GitHub App can (F-gh-fgpat-limits-01, F-gh-app-checks-01). For alerts from every repository, one App webhook carries events from every repository it is granted, where a personal account can have no webhook of its own (F-gh-webhook-types-01); a central poller or per-repository webhooks are the other ways (P-gh-cross-repo-alerts-01, grade C). Copies kept at Cloudflare would sit in its data centres (F-cf-loc-01). Whether to read through an App is open (below).
